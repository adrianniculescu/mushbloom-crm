What is RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)?

Definition & overview

Retrieval-augmented generation is an approach that retrieves relevant information from external sources and supplies it to a model when generating a response. It can provide current or specialised context without retraining the model.

Analogy & mental model

The assistant pulls the relevant handbook pages before answering your question.

Test whether the right evidence is retrieved and whether the answer actually follows it.

Why RAG matters

Business knowledge changes faster than model training. RAG can provide current policies and specialised evidence at answer time, while retaining references to the source. The weakest part may be retrieval rather than generation, so both stages need testing.

Practical business example

An internal assistant retrieves the relevant onboarding procedure and answers a staff question with a reference to the maintained document.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

How RAG works

  1. Search an approved collection for passages relevant to the user’s request.
  2. Supply the selected passages and source identifiers to the model.
  3. Generate and validate an evidence-based answer, escalating when evidence is missing.

Core components & mechanisms

  • Source collection
  • Retriever
  • Evidence-aware generation

Incorrect retrieval, stale documents or weak access filtering can still produce harmful answers.

RAG vs Fine-tuning

Fine-tuning changes model behaviour through training examples. RAG supplies external evidence during a request and is usually easier to refresh when facts change.
