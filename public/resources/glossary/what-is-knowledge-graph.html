What is Knowledge graph?

Definition & overview

A knowledge graph represents entities and relationships as connected data. Search systems can use such representations to distinguish people, businesses and concepts; publishing schema does not guarantee entry into a proprietary knowledge graph.

Analogy & mental model

It is a map showing who is connected to what, rather than a list of repeated keywords.

Keep identifying facts consistent and support relationships with credible sources.

Why Knowledge graph matters

Relationships make it easier to distinguish a person, company or product from similarly named entities. A knowledge graph organises those connections, but proprietary search graphs have their own inclusion processes. Businesses should focus on consistent facts and supporting sources rather than claiming schema creates a knowledge panel.

Practical business example

An organisation’s founder, services and official website are represented as connected facts rather than disconnected keyword strings.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

How Knowledge graph works

  1. Identify entities and their stable identifiers.
  2. Represent meaningful relationships and attributes with evidence.
  3. Keep relationships current and distinguish confirmed facts from uncertain associations.

Core components & mechanisms

  • Entities
  • Relationships
  • Attributes and evidence

A site’s own graph or schema does not guarantee inclusion in Google’s or another provider’s proprietary graph.

Knowledge graph vs Knowledge base

A knowledge base is a broader collection of information. A knowledge graph specifically represents entities and relationships; it may be one part of a knowledge system.
