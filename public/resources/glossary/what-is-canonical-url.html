What is Canonical URL?

Plain-text edition

Definition & overview

A canonical URL is the preferred address for a page when similar content is available at multiple URLs. A canonical tag expresses a preference to search engines; it is a signal, not an access restriction.

Analogy & mental model

It identifies the master edition when several copies of a document exist.

Use one consistent public domain and point each original page’s canonical at its own preferred URL.

Why Canonical URL matters

Duplicate addresses can split reporting and make it harder for systems to identify the preferred version of content. A consistent canonical strategy clarifies the intended master page. It should work alongside redirects, internal links and sitemaps rather than contradicting them.

Practical business example

A service page has tracking parameters from several campaigns, but all versions identify the clean service URL as the preferred address.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

How Canonical URL works

  1. Identify duplicates such as parameter variants or alternate hostnames.
  2. Choose the preferred public URL and declare it with a canonical link.
  3. Align internal links and sitemap entries, then inspect the engine’s chosen canonical.

Core components & mechanisms

  • Preferred URL
  • Canonical declaration
  • Consistent linking signals

Pointing every page to the homepage can erase the distinct identity of useful resource pages.

Canonical URL vs Redirect

A redirect moves a visitor to another address. A canonical tag leaves the current page accessible and signals a preferred indexed version; engines may choose differently.
