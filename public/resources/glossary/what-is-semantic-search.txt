# What is Semantic search?

Source: https://mushbloom.uk/resources/glossary/what-is-semantic-search.html

By Mushbloom. Published and updated: 2026-10-12.

## Definition & overview

Semantic search retrieves information using meaning and context rather than only exact text matches. Systems may combine semantic matching with keyword search and other relevance signals.

## Analogy & mental model

A librarian understands that “missed leads” and “unanswered enquiries” may describe the same problem.

Use natural language and clear topic coverage rather than stuffing repeated keyword variations.

## Why it matters

Exact wording is often an incomplete representation of a reader’s problem. Semantic retrieval can connect paraphrases and related concepts, but broad similarity can also blur important distinctions. Business systems should test relevance on actual tasks and exceptions.

## Practical business example

A search for “chasing unpaid invoices” finds the guide on “payment follow-up” but should not return unrelated sales-prospecting advice.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

## How it works

1. Represent or analyse the meaning of a query and candidate content.

2. Retrieve candidates using semantic relevance, often combined with keyword signals.

3. Filter and rank results according to the specific task and permitted source collection.

## Core components

- Query meaning

- Candidate retrieval

- Relevance assessment

Semantic similarity cannot by itself distinguish a true statement from a misleading one.

## Semantic search vs Embedding

An embedding is a representation often used in semantic search. Semantic search is the retrieval approach; it can involve additional signals and ranking stages.
