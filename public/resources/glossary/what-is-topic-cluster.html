What is Topic cluster?

Definition & overview

A topic cluster is an editorial organisation of a broad subject into a central page and linked supporting pages. The links help readers explore related questions; the structure alone does not establish expertise or rankings.

Analogy & mental model

A handbook has an overview chapter and focused chapters that refer to each other.

Assign each page a distinct reader need and avoid multiple pages competing to answer the same question.

Why Topic cluster matters

A coherent set of guides helps readers move from a broad question to a specific decision. The benefit comes from distinct, useful coverage and sensible connections. Publishing many thin pages under one topic is not equivalent to building expertise.

Practical business example

An AI automation hub links to guides on agent permissions, retrieval, approval and evaluation, each resolving a different implementation question.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

How Topic cluster works

  1. Choose a broad reader need and identify genuinely different supporting questions.
  2. Create an overview and focused pages that each contribute substantive information.
  3. Link pages contextually and update overlapping or obsolete material.

Core components & mechanisms

  • Overview page
  • Distinct supporting guides
  • Contextual internal links

A larger page count does not compensate for duplication, weak sourcing or missing original insight.

Topic cluster vs Keyword cannibalisation

A cluster gives pages distinct roles. Cannibalisation describes problematic overlap where multiple pages compete to fulfil essentially the same need.
