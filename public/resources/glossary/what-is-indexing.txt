# What is Indexing?

Source: https://mushbloom.uk/resources/glossary/what-is-indexing.html

By Mushbloom. Published and updated: 2026-10-12.

## Definition & overview

Indexing is the process of analysing and storing a page in a search engine’s index after discovery. A page can be crawlable but not indexed, and an indexed page is not guaranteed to rank or be cited.

## Analogy & mental model

The book has a catalogue entry; that does not mean it will be recommended to every reader.

Use search engine inspection tools to confirm actual index status rather than inferring it from a sitemap.

## Why it matters

A publicly accessible page may still be absent from a search index. That can explain low discovery, but index status must be checked rather than inferred. Indexing is also not a quality endorsement or a promise of visibility for commercially useful queries.

## Practical business example

A newly published guide appears in the sitemap but URL inspection reports that it has not yet been indexed. The owner investigates that status rather than treating submission as success.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

## How it works

1. The engine discovers and fetches a URL when its systems choose to crawl.

2. It analyses content, canonical signals and indexing directives.

3. It may store the preferred page and later select it for relevant searches.

## Core components

- Content analysis

- Canonical selection

- Index eligibility

Crawl requests and sitemap submission cannot guarantee inclusion or timing.

## Indexing vs Ranking

Indexing means a page is stored and eligible for consideration. Ranking is its selection and placement for a particular search.
