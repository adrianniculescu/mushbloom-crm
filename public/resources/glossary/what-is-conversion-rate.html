What is Conversion rate?

By Mushbloom · Published and updated 12 October 2026

Plain-text edition

Definition & overview

Conversion rate is the proportion of measured visits or interactions that complete a defined action. The denominator and action must be explicit; a booking-link click is not the same as a completed booking or a qualified enquiry.

Analogy & mental model

It is the share of visitors who take the agreed next step, not everyone who looks at the doorway.

Define qualified lead events and measure successful submissions separately from button clicks.

Why Conversion rate matters

Organic visits have business value only when the right readers can complete a useful next step. A clearly defined conversion reveals whether that step works. Mixing clicks, form starts and successful enquiries into one metric hides where the journey is failing.

Practical business example

A business tracks accepted enquiry submissions and later qualifies them, while reporting booking-link clicks separately from bookings confirmed by the scheduling service.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

How Conversion rate works

  1. Define the action and whether the denominator is users, sessions or another interaction.
  2. Record successful completion rather than only the intent to act.
  3. Review quality and source context, then compare like-for-like periods.

Core components & mechanisms

  • Defined action
  • Explicit denominator
  • Reliable completion event

A higher rate with a much smaller or irrelevant audience does not automatically mean more qualified leads.

Conversion rate vs Click-through rate

Click-through rate measures clicks relative to impressions or exposures. Conversion rate measures a defined completion relative to its declared starting population.
