# What is noindex?

Source: https://mushbloom.uk/resources/glossary/what-is-noindex.html

By Mushbloom. Published and updated: 2026-10-12.

## Definition & overview

noindex is a directive asking supporting search engines not to include a page in their index. They normally need to crawl the page to see it; blocking crawling can prevent them from reading the directive.

## Analogy & mental model

The book can be inspected, but its cover requests that it not appear in the public catalogue.

Use noindex for appropriate public-but-non-search pages, and real authentication for private information.

## Why it matters

Not every publicly accessible page belongs in search. A deliberate noindex directive can exclude low-value or administrative-adjacent public pages while leaving them usable. Private information still needs actual access controls, because indexing instructions are not secrecy.

## Practical business example

A public confirmation page uses noindex, while an admin workspace requires authentication regardless of crawler rules.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

## How it works

1. Choose pages that should remain accessible but excluded from supporting search indexes.

2. Serve the directive through a robots meta tag or supported HTTP header.

3. Allow the engine to fetch the directive and inspect whether the intended exclusion takes effect.

## Core components

- Indexing directive

- Crawl access to directive

- Status inspection

Do not apply a sitewide noindex when the intention is to exclude only one route.

## noindex vs robots.txt

robots.txt can stop compatible fetching. noindex asks for exclusion from an index and normally must be seen during fetching; blocking both can prevent the instruction from being read.
