What is Structured data?

Definition & overview

Structured data is machine-readable markup describing page content and relationships using a shared vocabulary such as Schema.org. It should reflect visible, accurate content and does not guarantee rich results or AI citations.

Analogy & mental model

It is a standardised label on the document, not extra evidence that the document is correct.

Choose a valid type, match the visible content and validate the markup before publishing.

Why Structured data matters

Consistent machine-readable descriptions can clarify what a page contains and how its entities relate. They work best when they reflect useful visible content. Incorrect or exaggerated markup introduces confusion rather than compensating for weak content.

Practical business example

A glossary uses DefinedTerm for definitions and a breadcrumb list for navigation, while an implementation guide uses Article for its editorial content.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

How Structured data works

  1. Choose a vocabulary and type appropriate to the page’s actual purpose.
  2. Describe visible facts and connect entities using consistent identifiers.
  3. Validate syntax and applicable requirements, then inspect what is actually served.

Core components & mechanisms

  • Vocabulary and type
  • Accurate properties
  • Validation

Valid markup does not guarantee rich results, better rankings or AI citations.

Structured data vs Page copy

Visible copy explains the subject to readers. Structured data describes that content in a standard form; it should not introduce claims absent from the page.
