# What is AEO (Answer Engine Optimisation)?

Source: https://mushbloom.uk/resources/glossary/what-is-aeo.html

By Mushbloom. Published and updated: 2026-10-12.

## Definition & overview

Answer engine optimisation is the practice of making information easier for answer systems to find, understand and use when responding to a question. It covers clear answers, reliable evidence and accessible pages, not a guaranteed placement.

## Analogy & mental model

AEO is like giving a helpful librarian a precise answer with the right reference attached.

Answer a real buyer question directly, then explain the evidence, limits and next step.

## Why it matters

Buyers increasingly ask complete questions rather than browse many supplier pages. A precise answer can help them understand your expertise before they contact you. The commercial value depends on answering a question that matters to your customers, not simply earning an isolated snippet.

## Practical business example

A bookkeeping business explains which documents a new client needs, then links to its onboarding service. The answer resolves uncertainty before the visitor makes contact.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

## How it works

1. Identify recurring questions from enquiries and sales calls, including objections and implementation concerns.

2. Write a direct answer first, then add the evidence, conditions and a concrete example.

3. Check that the page is accessible and measure whether readers continue to a relevant guide or service.

## Core components

- A specific reader question

- A self-contained answer with evidence

- A relevant next step

Answer formatting alone cannot make inaccurate or unoriginal content authoritative.

## AEO — Answer Engine Optimisation vs GEO

AEO emphasises answering questions; GEO emphasises usefulness in generated answers that may combine several sources. They overlap with ordinary SEO rather than replacing it.

# What is Agent2Agent (A2A)?

Source: https://mushbloom.uk/resources/glossary/what-is-a2a.html

By Mushbloom. Published and updated: 2026-10-12.

## Definition & overview

Agent2Agent is an open protocol for communication and collaboration between independent AI agent systems. It supports capability discovery and task exchanges without requiring agents to share their internal implementation.

## Analogy & mental model

It is a shared handover language for assistants working in different organisations.

Verify identity, task ownership and what information can cross an organisational boundary.

## Why it matters

Companies may use agents built by different providers. A shared task interface can reduce custom integration work, but it also creates new boundaries for identity and data sharing. Collaboration is only safe when ownership, permissions and completion criteria are clear.

## Practical business example

A research assistant requests a permitted summary from another specialist agent without obtaining that agent’s private prompts or underlying customer documents.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

## How it works

1. An agent discovers another agent’s declared capabilities through its published interface.

2. It sends an authorised task or message and receives status updates or requests for more input.

3. It receives the resulting artifact or task outcome and checks whether it meets the original requirements.

## Core components

- Capability description

- Tasks and status

- Messages and artifacts

Protocol compatibility is not permission to disclose confidential information or execute unrestricted tasks.

## Agent2Agent (A2A) vs MCP

A2A supports agent-to-agent task exchanges. MCP supports AI applications connecting to tools and data. They operate at different integration boundaries and can coexist.

## Frequently asked questions

# What is Agentic commerce?

Source: https://mushbloom.uk/resources/glossary/what-is-agentic-commerce.html

By Mushbloom. Published and updated: 2026-10-12.

## Definition & overview

Agentic commerce describes buying journeys in which AI agents help discover products, compare options or carry out authorised purchasing tasks. The degree of automation varies; payment, identity and consent still need explicit controls.

## Analogy & mental model

A shopping assistant can shortlist options, but must operate within the buyer’s agreed budget and authority.

Keep prices, availability and returns accurate, and make approval requirements explicit.

## Why it matters

AI assistance can change how customers compare and purchase products, but merchants still need trustworthy operational data. Accurate prices, availability and policies matter more than promotional wording. Businesses should distinguish recommendation-only experiences from workflows that can actually create orders or payments.

## Practical business example

An assistant shortlists compatible office supplies and prepares an order for the buyer to approve, rather than committing a company to unreviewed purchases.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

## How it works

1. The buyer defines a need and may specify constraints such as budget or delivery requirements.

2. An assistant finds and compares supported options using available product data.

3. Any purchase proceeds through an authorised merchant and payment process, with explicit controls on commitment.

## Core components

- Product discovery

- Comparison and buyer constraints

- Authorised purchasing

Do not assume all assistants, merchants or payment providers support the same capabilities.

## Agentic commerce vs ACP

Agentic commerce is the broad practice of agent-assisted buying. ACP is one specific checkout protocol; it does not cover every discovery channel, commercial negotiation or procurement process.

# What is Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP)?

Source: https://mushbloom.uk/resources/glossary/what-is-agentic-commerce-protocol.html

By Mushbloom. Published and updated: 2026-10-12.

## Definition & overview

Agentic Commerce Protocol is an open specification developed by OpenAI and Stripe for interactions between AI agents and merchants during checkout. It standardises commerce-related exchanges; it is not a general permission for agents to spend money independently.

## Analogy & mental model

It is a shared checkout form that an assistant and a shop can both understand.

Check merchant support, payment permissions and order approval before considering an agent-assisted purchase flow.

## Why it matters

Checkout is more consequential than recommending a product: it creates an order and may transfer money. ACP provides a shared transactional interface between an agent and a merchant. For a business, the priorities are reliable price and stock data, buyer consent and merchant-controlled fulfilment, not a claim that AI can negotiate or purchase anything automatically.

## Practical business example

A buyer asks an assistant to purchase a selected item from a supported store. The store supplies the total and terms, the buyer authorises the purchase, and the merchant handles fulfilment. This is an illustrative flow, not a claim that every store supports ACP.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

## How it works

1. The agent uses a supported merchant checkout interface to create or update a checkout session with selected items.

2. The merchant returns authoritative totals, availability and information required to complete the order.

3. The buyer’s authorised payment and confirmation flow completes the session; the merchant remains responsible for the order and fulfilment.

## Core components

- Checkout sessions and line items

- Merchant-provided totals and order state

- Authorised payment exchange

Implementation and availability evolve. ACP does not grant unlimited spending authority or guarantee inclusion in an assistant’s recommendations.

## Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP) vs Schema markup

Schema markup describes products and other page content. ACP defines transactional exchanges for checkout; adding Product schema does not create an ACP checkout integration. MCP connects tools, while A2A connects agent systems.

What is a sensible first step for a small business?
Check merchant support, payment permissions and order approval before considering an agent-assisted purchase flow. The agent uses a supported merchant checkout interface to create or update a checkout session with selected items.

# What is AI agent?

Source: https://mushbloom.uk/resources/glossary/what-is-ai-agent.html

By Mushbloom. Published and updated: 2026-10-12.

## Definition & overview

An AI agent is a software system that uses a model to choose actions and interact with tools toward a defined goal. Its access, instructions and approval boundaries determine what it can safely do.

## Analogy & mental model

Think of a junior assistant with a task list and a restricted set of keys—not an unsupervised employee.

Define the agent’s role, approved data, tools and escalation rules before connecting business accounts.

## Why it matters

Agents become useful when a task needs more than one fixed response, such as checking information and selecting a next action. They also introduce operational risks because a wrong action can affect customers or records. A defined role is more valuable than an impressive claim of autonomy.

## Practical business example

An enquiry agent categorises a request and drafts a reply. A staff member approves a price exception rather than letting the agent invent a discount.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

## How it works

1. Give the agent a narrow goal and the minimum tools needed to complete it.

2. Let the model propose an action, then validate its arguments and permissions outside the model.

3. Record the result and route exceptions or sensitive actions to a person.

## Core components

- Role and goal

- Tool access and action validation

- Approval and audit trail

A natural-language instruction is not a substitute for a server-enforced permission.

## AI agent vs Workflow automation

A fixed workflow follows predefined branches. An agent can select actions within those boundaries; the workflow still controls permissions, approvals and handoffs.

# What is AI citation rate?

Source: https://mushbloom.uk/resources/glossary/what-is-citation-rate.html

By Mushbloom. Published and updated: 2026-10-12.

## Definition & overview

AI citation rate is a defined measurement of how often a target source is linked in a sampled set of AI answers. Its meaning depends on the denominator, prompt set, engines and collection method; there is no universal formula.

## Analogy & mental model

It is the proportion of sampled reports that include your source in their footnotes.

Choose a documented denominator, deduplicate runs and separate links from brand mentions.

## Why it matters

A percentage is only informative when its denominator is clear. Citation rate can show patterns within a defined test sample, but different prompts or engines may give very different results. It should support diagnosis rather than suggest a universal score for a brand.

## Practical business example

A visibility report separates answers containing a link to the target domain from answers that merely mention the company, and states exactly which buyer questions were tested.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

## How it works

1. Define the sampled answers, target domain and what counts as a citation.

2. Count qualifying linked references and the agreed total of sampled responses.

3. Report the fraction with dates, engine settings, repetitions and exclusions.

## Core components

- Defined denominator

- Linked-source rule

- Sampling documentation

A small or selectively chosen prompt set can produce an impressive but unrepresentative percentage.

## AI citation rate vs Share of Model

Citation rate specifically concerns linked references under a declared counting rule. Share of Model can include brand presence relative to other brands and needs its own definition.

## Frequently asked questions

What does AI citation rate mean?

How is it different from Share of Model?

What is a sensible first step for a small business?

# What is AI evaluation?

Source: https://mushbloom.uk/resources/glossary/what-is-evaluation.html

By Mushbloom. Published and updated: 2026-10-12.

## Definition & overview

AI evaluation is the systematic testing of model or agent behaviour against defined requirements. It can examine factual accuracy, retrieval quality, tool use, safety, cost and task completion using representative cases.

## Analogy & mental model

It is a driving test with realistic routes, not one successful trip around the car park.

Keep a repeatable test set covering normal cases, missing evidence and unsafe requests.

## Why it matters

One successful demonstration does not show that a system is dependable. Evaluation exposes recurring errors, missing evidence and unsafe actions before wider deployment. The cases should reflect real user tasks and commercial consequences rather than only easy examples.

## Practical business example

An enquiry assistant is tested on routine requests, spam, missing contact details and demands for unsupported pricing before it is allowed to assist staff.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

## How it works

1. Define measurable requirements and a representative set of expected outcomes.

2. Run the workflow against normal, ambiguous, failure and adversarial cases.

3. Inspect errors, revise the system and repeat the same tests after changes.

## Core components

- Representative cases

- Explicit success criteria

- Repeatable comparison

Automated judges can be useful but should not replace human review of consequential or disputed cases.

## AI evaluation vs Monitoring

Evaluation tests defined cases before or between releases. Monitoring observes behaviour in use; both are needed because production inputs may differ from the test set.

# What is AI guardrails?

Source: https://mushbloom.uk/resources/glossary/what-is-guardrails.html

By Mushbloom. Published and updated: 2026-10-12.

## Definition & overview

AI guardrails are controls that limit inputs, outputs or actions in an AI system. They can include access checks, spending limits, output validation and human approval; prompting alone is not a reliable security boundary.

## Analogy & mental model

They are the rails, brakes and operating rules around a machine—not a note asking it to be careful.

Enforce critical restrictions outside the model and test attempts to bypass them.

## Why it matters

Business risk comes from what an AI system can disclose or change, not only what it says. Guardrails place enforceable boundaries around those consequences. They should be designed from concrete risks and tested with adversarial cases rather than treated as a reassuring slogan.

## Practical business example

An agent may draft a customer email, but a separate policy check rejects attachments containing another client’s private records.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

## How it works

1. Identify sensitive data, actions and failure conditions in the workflow.

2. Add independent access checks, validation, restricted tools and approval gates.

3. Test attempts to bypass controls and monitor both blocked and successful actions.

## Core components

- Access enforcement

- Input and output validation

- Approval and monitoring

No single control eliminates all risk; permissions and data isolation must remain effective independently of model output.

## AI guardrails vs Prompt instruction

Instructions influence model behaviour. Enforced guardrails restrict what the application can actually do even when the model ignores or misunderstands instructions.

## Frequently asked questions

# What is AI Overviews?

Source: https://mushbloom.uk/resources/glossary/what-is-ai-overviews.html

By Mushbloom. Published and updated: 2026-10-12.

## Definition & overview

AI Overviews are Google Search features that produce AI-generated summaries for some searches and may include links to supporting web pages. Availability and source selection depend on the query and Google’s systems.

## Analogy & mental model

Instead of only a shelf of books, the librarian gives a summary and points to selected books.

Maintain normal SEO eligibility and useful text; there is no special markup that guarantees inclusion.

## Why it matters

A summary can answer part of a search before the user reaches a website. That changes where discovery happens, but a cited page still needs to offer deeper value. Businesses should treat summary visibility as one observation, not proof that qualified traffic or revenue increased.

## Practical business example

A software buyer sees an overview of integration options and follows a source to inspect detailed limitations and implementation advice.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

## How it works

1. Google assesses a query and whether an AI summary is appropriate.

2. Its systems retrieve relevant information and generate a response with selected links.

3. Users may inspect those links or continue searching; source inclusion can vary between runs.

## Core components

- Query eligibility

- Retrieved web evidence

- Summary and supporting links

Google does not require a special AI schema or an llms.txt file for these features.

## AI Overviews vs Featured snippet

A featured snippet extracts content from a selected page. An AI Overview generates a synthesis that may rely on several sources and is not the same search feature.

# What is AI referral traffic?

Source: https://mushbloom.uk/resources/glossary/what-is-referral-traffic.html

By Mushbloom. Published and updated: 2026-10-12.

## Definition & overview

AI referral traffic is website traffic attributed to visits from AI products or their linked answers. Some referrals lack identifiable source information, so analytics may undercount them or classify them as direct traffic.

## Analogy & mental model

It counts people who arrive through a recommendation, when the introduction is still attached.

Measure landing pages and qualified enquiries alongside identifiable AI referrers.

## Why it matters

Being quoted is not the same as receiving a useful visit. Referral data helps connect visibility with reader behaviour, but source identification can be incomplete. Landing pages and successful enquiries provide additional evidence without assuming every direct visit came from AI.

## Practical business example

A guide receives identifiable visits from an AI answer. The business checks whether those readers explore the matching service and successfully send an enquiry.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

## How it works

1. Capture available session-source information under the site’s consent rules.

2. Identify known AI referrers and inspect their landing-page behaviour.

3. Connect visits to successfully completed lead actions while acknowledging unattributed traffic.

## Core components

- Session attribution

- Landing page

- Completed lead event

Analytics cannot reliably identify every AI-origin visit, particularly across copied links or privacy restrictions.

## AI referral traffic vs Direct traffic

Direct traffic often means no usable source was available. It can include many origins and should not be automatically reclassified as AI traffic.

By Mushbloom. Published and updated: 2026-10-12.

## Definition & overview

AI search combines information retrieval with AI-generated answers. A system may search the web or a knowledge collection, select evidence and compose a response; not every model answer uses live search.

A researcher collects documents before writing a briefing rather than answering from memory alone.

Separate live-search citations from model-only brand mentions when measuring visibility.

## Why it matters

AI search can surface information through answers rather than only lists of links. It is useful to distinguish discovery, citation, referral traffic and enquiry generation, because these are different stages. A company can be mentioned frequently yet receive no relevant commercial visits.

## Practical business example

An owner compares ways to automate quote follow-up. A helpful source explains both the workflow and cases where a human must still intervene.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

## How it works

1. The system interprets the request and may expand it into related searches.

2. It retrieves and selects passages from available sources.

3. The model composes an answer, sometimes with links that users can follow.

## Core components

- Search or retrieval layer

- Source selection

- Generated answer

Not every AI product searches on every request, and a fluent answer is not evidence of live retrieval.

## AI search vs Model-only response

A model-only response can draw on learned patterns without live retrieval. AI search introduces retrieved evidence, although that evidence may still be misread.

# What is Backlink?

Source: https://mushbloom.uk/resources/glossary/what-is-backlink.html

By Mushbloom. Published and updated: 2026-10-12.

## Definition & overview

A backlink is a link from another website to a page on your website. Relevant editorial links can help discovery and contribute to search signals; a link is not proof of expertise by itself.

## Analogy & mental model

It is a reference from another publication, rather than a claim printed in your own brochure.

Earn links through useful original work and genuine partnerships, not purchased ranking schemes.

## Why it matters

External links can help people and crawlers discover a useful resource. Their value depends on relevance, context and editorial credibility. A genuine link from an industry association can bring appropriate visitors, while a large number of unrelated links may do little for the business.

## Practical business example

A trade association links to an agency’s documented automation checklist because members need a practical starting point.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

## How it works

1. Publish something another organisation has a legitimate reason to reference.

2. Earn a contextual link with descriptive text from a relevant page.

3. Check the linked destination, referral quality and any changes to the referring page.

## Core components

- Referring page

- Link destination

- Editorial context

Manipulative link schemes can violate search policies; paid links should not be presented as independent endorsements.

## Backlink vs Brand mention

A backlink provides a navigable connection to a page. A mention can establish context without linking, and neither should be counted automatically as a lead.

- Engine-specific diagnostics

# What is Tool calling?

Source: https://mushbloom.uk/resources/glossary/what-is-tool-calling.html

By Mushbloom. Published and updated: 2026-10-12.

## Definition & overview

Tool calling lets a model request an operation through a defined software interface, such as retrieving a record or drafting a calendar event. The application decides whether to execute the request and returns the result.

## Analogy & mental model

The assistant fills out a work order; a controlled service decides whether to perform it.

Validate arguments, permissions and side effects before executing any model-proposed operation.

## Why it matters

A model cannot safely be given direct authority simply because it can express a function request. Tool calling separates the proposal from the execution layer. That separation lets a business enforce validation, limits and approval while still using AI to interpret requests.

## Practical business example

An assistant requests an available appointment slot. The calendar service returns real availability; the assistant cannot invent a booking that the service never accepted.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

## How it works

1. Describe a tool with clear arguments and permitted purpose.

2. Receive a model-generated request and validate it against the user’s rights and business rules.

3. Execute only an authorised operation and return its actual result to the model.

## Core components

- Tool schema

- Application-side validation

- Execution result

Never treat model-provided identifiers, amounts or destinations as inherently trusted.

## Tool calling vs Autonomous action

A tool request is not necessarily an executed action. The application remains responsible for whether and how the request is carried out.

By Mushbloom. Published and updated: 2026-10-12.

## Definition & overview

A vector database stores numerical representations such as embeddings and supports similarity retrieval. It can help retrieve related content, but access filtering, source quality and relevance testing remain separate responsibilities.

## Analogy & mental model

It is a searchable map of nearby ideas, not a fact-checker or permission system.

Apply client and document permissions before returning retrieval results to a model.

## Why it matters

Large collections need efficient ways to retrieve related material. Vector databases support that task, but similarity retrieval can expose the wrong document if access filters are weak. Treat retrieval permissions as a business requirement, not an optional model instruction.

## Practical business example

A client-specific assistant retrieves only the current client’s approved records even when another client has a semantically similar document.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

## How it works

1. Create compatible embeddings for approved documents and retain source metadata.

2. Store them with client, version and access identifiers.

3. Query for similar items while applying permissions, then verify the returned evidence.

## Core components

- Vectors and indexes

- Source metadata

- Permission-aware query

A vector store is not a truth database, and retrieval filters must not rely on the model remembering which client it serves.

## Vector database vs Traditional database

A traditional database often retrieves by explicit fields and relationships. A vector database adds similarity search; business systems commonly need both capabilities.

- Actions and branching

- Exceptions and ownership

# What is Zero-click search?

Source: https://mushbloom.uk/resources/glossary/what-is-zero-click-search.html

By Mushbloom. Published and updated: 2026-10-12.

## Definition & overview

A zero-click search is a search interaction that ends without a click through to an external website. The user may find an answer on the results page, refine the query or abandon the task; it does not always indicate satisfaction.

## Analogy & mental model

Someone reads the notice in a shop window and leaves without entering.

Give people a reason to visit for deeper evidence, tools or a relevant service—not just a repeated short answer.

## Why it matters

Search answers can resolve simple questions without sending a visitor to the source site. That does not make all informational content useless, but it changes the role of a basic definition. A site needs deeper evidence, decision support or an appropriate service for readers who continue.

## Practical business example

A user learns the expansion of an acronym without visiting a website. Another user follows a guide because they need implementation details, risks and examples.

Illustrative scenario, not a client case study or promised result.

## How it works

1. A user searches and encounters an answer or useful information within the search interface.

2. The user does not follow a link to an external site in that interaction.

3. The session may end, continue with another query or proceed through another channel.

## Core components

- Search interaction

- On-interface information

- No external click

No click does not establish that the user found a satisfactory answer or remembered the cited brand.

A citation can be displayed in a zero-click interaction. Citation visibility and referral traffic are separate outcomes and should not be conflated.
